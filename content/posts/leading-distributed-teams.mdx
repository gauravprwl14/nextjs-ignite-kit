# Leading Distributed Engineering Teams

Managing distributed teams is fundamentally different from co-located teams. The playbooks that work in an office fail across time zones.

## The Communication Tax

Every distributed team pays a communication tax. The question is: how do you minimize it?

### Async-First Communication

Default to asynchronous communication:

- **Documentation over meetings** - Write it down
- **RFC processes** - Structured technical decisions
- **Video recordings** - For complex topics

### Strategic Synchronous Time

Reserve real-time communication for:

- Critical incidents
- One-on-ones
- Team bonding
- Complex technical discussions

## Time Zone Management

With 5+ time zones, you can't have everyone in every meeting.

### The Golden Hours

Identify overlapping hours and protect them:

- Use for critical syncs only
- Rotate meeting times to share the burden
- Record everything for async consumption

## Building Trust Remotely

Trust is harder to build without water cooler conversations:

### What Works

1. **Virtual coffee chats** - Scheduled informal time
2. **Working sessions** - Pair programming across zones
3. **Transparency** - Share context, not just decisions
4. **Recognition** - Public appreciation in async channels

## Documentation as a Superpower

In distributed teams, documentation isn't nice-to-have—it's infrastructure:

- **Decision logs** - Why we chose X over Y
- **Architecture diagrams** - Updated, not dusty
- **Runbooks** - For every production scenario

## The Bottom Line

Distributed teams require **intentional** leadership. What happens naturally in an office must be designed deliberately when remote.
